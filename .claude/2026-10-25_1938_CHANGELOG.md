# Stage 6c — Clarity: change log

Branch `worktree-stage6c-clarity`. Plan and per-task reports:
`.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-20-vocalis-stage6c-clarity/`.

Stage 6b (the previous stage) made long readings play without gaps or
restarts. Stage 6c is about making those readings sound *better* — clearer
voice quality, a steadier AI voice, a helpful warning if you pick a voice
that doesn't match the language it's reading, and speech that reads things
like web addresses and money amounts the way a person would say them out
loud, instead of reading the punctuation marks literally.

## What changed, and why

1. **Each voice now controls its own speed, instead of one shared
   "stretch" step.** Before this stage, Vocalis sped up or slowed down
   *every* voice's speech using one generic audio-stretching trick after
   the voice had already spoken. That trick works, but it is a bit lossy
   — it can make speech sound slightly artificial, especially for voices
   that already have their own proper way of speaking faster or slower.
   Now:
   - **Kokoro** and **Piper** (two of the three voices) use their own
     built-in speed controls, the same way a music player might have a
     "playback speed" dial built in, instead of us stretching the
     recording afterward. This sounds cleaner.
   - **Qwen3-TTS** (the main, highest-quality voice) has no such built-in
     dial, so it still uses the old stretching trick — but only gently,
     and only to speed things up a little (never more than 1.2x faster,
     and never slower than its normal pace). Pushing it further than that
     started to hurt how natural it sounded, so we capped it.
   - The Speaking Speed slider in Settings still works the same way for
     you — you just won't hear any quality loss on Kokoro or Piper
     anymore, and Qwen3-TTS will simply stay at its normal pace instead of
     sounding strained if you ask for something outside its safe range.

2. **Qwen3-TTS (the main voice) was tuned to sound steadier and generate
   faster.** We adjusted some of its internal "creativity" settings to be
   a bit more conservative, and we stopped asking it to generate more
   speech than a given piece of text actually needs. In practice this
   means it's less likely to ramble, repeat itself, or wander off near the
   end of a long sentence, and it produces speech a bit faster too.

3. **A friendly warning if you pick a non-English voice for Qwen3-TTS.**
   Qwen3-TTS offers several named voices, but only two of them (`Ryan` and
   `Aiden`) are actually native English speakers — the rest are native to
   Chinese, Japanese or Korean. Since Vocalis currently only asks it to
   speak English, picking one of those other voices still works, but
   sounds a bit off. Now, if you pick one of those, you'll see a warning
   in the logs so you know why it might sound a little strange. It never
   stops you from using it — it's just a heads-up.

4. **Web links, money and percent signs are now spoken properly.** Before
   this stage, if your document or copied text contained something like
   "https://example.com" or "$42" or "50%", Vocalis would try to read the
   punctuation out loud, which sounds odd. Now it recognizes these
   patterns and speaks them the way a person would say them — for example,
   "example dot com" instead of reading the slashes and dots literally,
   "42 dollars" instead of "dollar sign 42", and "50 percent" instead of
   "50 percent-sign". The `&` and `@` symbols are also spoken as "and" and
   "at". This happens automatically for both Quick Reader (copy and read)
   and Document Reader (open and read a file) — you don't need to turn
   anything on. Plain numbers are left untouched, since the voices already
   read numbers correctly on their own.

## A test gap we found and fixed along the way

While running the final full check for this stage, one older test failed:
it checked that changing the Speaking Speed slider actually changed how
the voice sounded, using a quick stand-in ("fake") version of the Qwen3-TTS
voice instead of the real one. That stand-in hadn't been updated to know
about change #1 above (each voice now paces itself) — it was still
pretending the *old* "stretch everything afterward" approach was in use.
This was not a bug in the real Vocalis code; it was just an old test that
hadn't caught up with the redesign. We updated that one test so its
stand-in voice behaves like the real voices now do (reading its assigned
speed live, the same way Qwen3-TTS/Kokoro/Piper do internally), without
touching any of the actual Vocalis code. Nothing else about how Vocalis
behaves for you changed because of this fix — it only fixes how that one
test checks things.
